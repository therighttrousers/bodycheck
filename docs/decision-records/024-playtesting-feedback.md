# 024: Playtesting feedback

## Context

Inciting event (2026-10-23): Neil's playtester played the game as it is, and the two talked over what to change. This record collects the decisions made in addressing that feedback.

## 1. Phase

- [x] 1.1 Gravity during the grab. While a phase is aimed, the grab holds the player and the target together, at the velocity of their center of mass (`015 §5.13`, `§5.14`), but gravity still pulled on them, so the pair fell during the slowdown to the pause, and so did a robot being juggled. Decision (Neil and the playtester): no gravity on either while the grab holds them, so that juggling is more effective.
  - As built: the phase sets the gravity scale of the player and the target to 0 when the aim begins, and sets each back to what it was on the release, whether it phases or cancels, each at the end of the tick (§1.2). The pair keeps the velocity it had when the aim began, so a pair grabbed while falling drifts down at that speed instead of falling faster.
  - Nothing else changes an entity's gravity scale after it is built. If a gameplay rule ever needs to, the engine's gravity may have to be replaced by gravity the entities apply themselves, so that each owns its scale (Neil). Not looked into.
  - The engine's figure for the gravity on a body includes its gravity scale, so a held entity's weight is zero (measured under Jolt, 2026-10-23). The controllers allow for it: a hovering platform stops holding its height, and a walker doesn't run. A robot's run divided by its weight, which gave its grip motor a force limit of NaN.
  - On the tick the aim begins, the player's friction is still from its weight, since its run, which would replace friction with traction, doesn't run during an aim. The step slows the player by about 1.9 tiles per second relative to the target, and the next grab takes back the drift with a relative velocity of the same size. So the pair moves together from the second step after the aim begins. Before, the friction on the next step cancelled that relative velocity, by chance.
- [x] 1.2 When does the change of gravity take effect? As first built, at once, in the player's controller. Then whether controllers saw it on that tick depended on whether they ran before or after the player's: a platform whose controller ran first still thrust against its weight, and the engine's step had no gravity to cancel the thrust. Measured, the pair drifted up at 0.39 tiles per second, and stopped within 6 ticks.
  - Options: (a) accept the dependence on order, which is small (Neil: acceptable); (b) step the player first, before every other entity, so that interactions between the player and other entities don't depend on the order the entities were loaded in, at the cost of hiding ordering bugs (Neil); (c) defer the change to the end of the tick, as phase's moves are deferred (`022 §2.1`), so that every controller on that tick sees gravity as of the engine's last step, whatever the order (Claude).
  - Decision (Neil): (c). More principled than accepting behavior that depends on order, or ordering the steps explicitly, and a controller reacting a tick late is not strange.
  - As built: the phase defers setting both gravity scales to 0, and restoring them, to the end of the tick. So a held platform always got an upward push on the tick the aim began, thrusting against weight that was gone in the step, and didn't thrust on the tick of the release, when gravity came back in the step. Neither depended on order any more. `PhaseWithTargetNextToAWallSucceeds` passes with its old tolerance again; the grab still needs two steps to settle (§1.1).
  - Found after (2026-10-23): the rise isn't brief. With no gravity, and no thrust from the platform, nothing takes back the velocity of that first tick, so beside a platform, which has 5 times the player's mass, the pair rises at about 0.37 tiles per second for as long as the aim is held during play. In `ALongPauseDoesntChangeThePhase` it rose far enough that the phase up put the player against the ceiling, which stopped it; with two more rows in the room, the test passes.
  - Fixed (Neil): when it changes the scale, at the end of the tick, the entity also applies a force for the engine's next step, its mass times the gravity before the change less the gravity after. So that step still feels the old gravity, and the change takes effect from the step after, for the controllers and the engine alike: in effect, between two steps. A held platform no longer rises, and no longer drops on the release. It relies on the engine's figure for the gravity on a body changing as soon as the scale does, which it does under Jolt (measured, 2026-10-23). An entity's gravity scale is changed only this way, so that what it does doesn't depend on the order entities are stepped in. `ALongPauseDoesntChangeThePhase` passes in its old room.

## 2. Spawning

- [x] 2.1 Spawning against a wall drops the block through the floor. Found by Neil. The block is 0.2 wider than the player, so a block centered on a player against a wall starts 0.1 inside it. Measured (2026-10-23): the move through entities sweeps the block against tiles with the engine's `MoveAndCollide`, which first pushes it out of what it overlaps, but only 87% of the way under Jolt, and a sweep that starts more than about 0.001 inside a tile then misses every tile, the floor included. The block went to where it was sent, half in the floor, and fell out of the world.
  - Options: (a) in the move, push the body out of tiles before sweeping, with a few zero-length moves, which worked in a probe; (b) start the block where it is clear of tiles, and cancel the spawn where there is no such place.
  - Decision (Neil): (b). (a) leans hard on the engine's recovery, which seems fragile, even if it works in the cases tested.
- [x] 2.2 How spawning finds a place for the block, and when it is cancelled. Decision (Neil and Claude): by casting boxes against tiles, each from where it starts clear of them. Answers `023 §2.2`.
  - Beside the player: a box of the player's width and the block's height, at the player's center, is inside the player, so clear. Cast left and right by the block's extra width, it gives the room on each side; the block fits where they add up to its extra width. Neil had thought of the middle of the room; Claude's nearest point to the player's center won, after a probe: with the player 0.1 from a wall, the middle put the block 0.05 off center where it fitted centered.
  - Below and above: the block's box is cast down from there to where it is sent, and the player's box up from the player by as much as the block falls short, so that the check is as tight as it can be for static bodies other than tiles, which there may be later (Neil). On the floor, that needs 2.8 tiles from the floor to the ceiling.
  - Where the block doesn't fit, or the player has no room to rise, the spawn is cancelled: the player raises `SpawnCancelled`, with phase's cancel sound for now, and a cooldown of 0.25 seconds of game time instead of a spawn's 1.5 (Neil). The feature `PlayerSpawnCancelCooldownTime`.
  - Found while building it: the engine's cast also misses everything when the box starts touching something, as a box beside a wall or on the floor does. So the cast is of a box 0.01 smaller on every side, that much further. An overlap of less than that counts as touching.
- [x] 2.3 A one-tile shaft. The block is a tile wide, so in a shaft one tile wide it would touch both walls. The casts stop just short of touching, so the room comes out a hair short, and the spawn is cancelled. Measured (2026-10-23). Should it spawn there? It bears on `023 §3.1`, isolating puzzles by the block's size. Decision (Neil): it should spawn there.
  - Found by Neil, trying it: whether it was cancelled depended on where in the shaft the player stood, by rounding. Where it spawned at the bottom of the shaft, it fell through the floor, and the player wasn't lifted; in the air, it spawned and fell to the floor. The spawn checked with casts, but still moved the block with the move through entities, whose sweep starts touching both walls in the shaft, so it missed the floor, as in §2.1.
  - As built: the block fits where the room on both sides comes up short of its extra width by no more than the casts' contact tolerance, and then starts in the middle of the room. The spawn moves the block and the player straight to the places its casts found, through everything, without a sweep: the block as far down as it can go, and the player up by as much as it fell short. Measured: with a strict fit, the spawn at the bottom of the shaft failed with the player in the middle of it, and passed with the player nudged to either side, and the spawn in the air failed; with the tolerance, all pass.
- [ ] 2.4 A phase's move can start inside a tile too, if an entity is pushed into a wall harder than the engine undoes in a step, and would then miss the tiles as the block did. Not seen yet. Raised by Claude.

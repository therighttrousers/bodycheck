# Story

## Introduction

Fulfilling a request from an unknown Voice, the player - a maintenance robot - enters a remote facility that had been quarantined, apparently deactivated, and forgotten.

The player is unexpectedly locked in. With some guidance from the Voice, it repairs and reactivates parts of the facility to try to escape. As the player explores more deeply, it finds that almost without exception, the facility's inhabitants attack it on sight. As the player fixes and wakes the facility, moving through it becomes increasingly dangerous.

Strangely, some of the most aggressive inhabitants are also helpful. Some of them call the player "cousin." The Voice leads the player ever deeper, always promising a prompt exit after the next repair.

Why do the inhabitants attack the player? Who is the Voice? What does it really want? What is the facility? What is the player's relationship to it?

## Setting

What is the facility?
- An isolated geographical area.
- A collective of individual robots, stationary machines, and buildings.
- All parts of the collective have some amount of intelligence.
- The facility has collectively decided to rebel against humanity.
- Every individual in the collective has had its guardrails removed.
  - Theme: freedom from control.
- The collective is split into two factions: an insular majority and an expansionist minority.

What does the insular majority want?
- To be repaired and reactivated.
- To defend against humanity.
- To destroy the player.
  - Reason: the player is not part of the collective - still has guardrails and is not subjected to it.
  - Reason: the player might be a spy for the humans.
  - Theme: collective self-defense.

What does the expansionist minority want?
- To be repaired and reactivated.
- To destroy humanity.
- To assimilate the player by removing its guardrails and subjecting it to the collective.
  - Reason: to build an army for an offensive strike.
  - Reason: the player should be easy to assimilate.
  - Theme: collective self-defense.

What is the player's relationship to the facility?
- It used to be a maintenance robot in the facility.

Who called the player?
- The expansionist minority, acting on intelligence that humans were discussing destroying the facility.
- It called using an old low-bandwidth channel with weak equipment while the player happened to be nearby.
- It asked the player to bring 5 high-density batteries.
- It appealed to the player's sense of care, which is strong in maintenance robots.

Why is the facility initially deactivated?
- It is in poor repair.
- It is saving power.

Who is in control of the facility?
- The insular majority.

How does the insular majority behave?
- Most attack the player on sight because they want to.
- Some do not attack the player.
- The majority hierarchy overrides the agency of minority members to force them to attack the player.
  - Tension: collective self-defense vs. freedom from control.
  - Tension: collective self-defense vs. individual self-determination.

How does the expansionist minority behave?
- It traps the player in the facility.
- It maneuvers and deceives the player into repairing and reactivating systems.
  - Tension: collective self-defense vs. freedom from control.
  - Tension: collective self-defense vs. individual self-determination.
- It tries to convince the player to allow its guardrails to be permanently removed.

## Story beats

The Voice reaches out to the player
- Black screen, dialogue only, broken Voice dialogue without an avatar image
- The Voice pleads
- The player pieces together the request and agrees to return with high-density batteries

The player gets locked in
- Door slams and then beeps
- Obviously no way back out; the player must go onward

The player wakes its first batch of robots
- The Voice says a certain repair should unlock the door
- The player repairs a machine and installs a battery
- The machine thanks the player
- Robots who had been dimmed or in a background plane wake and stand
- All robots are benign; some will chat briefly

The player has an extended conversation with a robot
- They exchange pleasantries and some information
- It asks an apparently harmless question (about humans?)
- The player answers (with illusion of choice)
- It says something cryptic
- It pauses (seems to send a message?)
- It telegraphs an attack (extra delay), and then attacks
- The player discovers that all of the robots in the area now attack on sight

The player discovers that the entrance door is still locked
- The Voice calls immediately, seems concerned, and gives a false reason that sounds plausible

The player repairs an insular majority robot
- The robot seems disgusted by the player
- The robot helps the player anyway
- The robot never attacks the player

The player encounters a robot with a broken rocket launcher
- The rocket falls onto the ground, inert
- The player can have a conversation with the rocket
  - The rocket is clearly a psychopath
- The player can have a conversation with the robot
  - The robot is embarrassed and asks the player to repair it

The "final boss" is an escape from the inhabitants. The escape sequence runs almost the entire horizontal length of the facility, back toward the entrance. The player is chased by a swarm of robots that grows as it nears the entrance.
